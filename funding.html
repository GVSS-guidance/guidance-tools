OSAP

Understanding OSAP before you apply

The part most families find confusing, in plain terms.

Why this tool does not calculate your OSAP. Ontario does not publish the tables it uses — income thresholds and the grant/loan formula are not public, and every assessment is worked out individually. Any figure we produced here would be a guess, and a guess is a bad reason to accept or turn down an offer. Use the official estimator below; it is the only source that reflects your actual circumstances.

Open the official OSAP Aid Estimator ↗
Grant or loan — what is the difference?

An OSAP assessment usually returns both.

  • Grant — you keep it. Nothing to repay.
  • Loan — repaid once you finish, and it does not accrue interest while you are studying full-time.

Enter them separately above. They are both money you can spend this year, but only one of them follows you afterwards, and that matters when you compare pathways.

Will my parents' income be counted?

Usually yes, if you are applying straight out of high school. OSAP treats most students as dependent, which means household income forms part of the assessment even if your family is not contributing anything.

You are generally assessed as independent if you have been out of high school four or more years, have worked full-time for two years, are married or have children of your own. The definitions are exact, so check them rather than assuming.

If your family situation is complicated — estrangement, a parent who will not share income details, a recent change in circumstances — speak to a counsellor. There are review processes, and they are not obvious from the website.

When do I apply, and what will I need?

Apply before you are admitted. You do not need an offer in hand, and applying early means money arrives when tuition is due rather than months later. Applications for the following school year usually open in the spring.

Have ready:

  • Your Social Insurance Number
  • Your parents' or guardians' income information, if you are assessed as dependent
  • The school and program you are applying to (an estimate is fine; you can change it)
  • Your own income and savings

You will need to confirm your enrolment once term starts before funds are released.

Does OSAP cover living costs, or only tuition?

The assessment considers your whole cost of attendance — tuition, fees, books, and living expenses — not just the tuition line. That is why the figure from the Cost Explorer is a useful thing to have in front of you when you use the estimator.

What it will actually fund depends on your assessment, which is why it is an input here rather than something we predict.

Awards

Scholarships and bursaries

Two different things, applied for in two different ways.

Entrance scholarships — most are automatic

This is the piece students most often miss. At nearly every Ontario university, the main entrance scholarship is awarded automatically on your admission average — there is no separate application. You are considered when you are admitted.

Thresholds vary by school and change year to year, but the shape is consistent: awards typically begin somewhere in the 80s and increase in bands as the average rises. A few points can be worth thousands of dollars a year.

Check the entrance-award page of each school you are applying to, because the renewable ones matter most — some continue every year if you maintain a minimum average, others are one-time only. That distinction is easy to miss and worth several thousand dollars over a degree.

External scholarships — where to look

These are awarded by organisations rather than schools, and they do need applications and usually references, so start early.

  • ScholarshipsCanada — the largest Canadian database, searchable by profile.
  • StudentAwards — similar, worth registering with both.
  • Loran Award — among the largest in Canada, judged on character and service rather than marks alone. Applications typically close in the autumn of Grade 12.
  • Schulich Leader Scholarships — for STEM, nominated through your school, so speak to guidance early.
  • TD Scholarships for Community Leadership — recognises sustained community involvement.

Ask your counsellor about school-nominated awards. Some of the largest require a nomination from Georges Vanier rather than a direct application, so they never appear in a public search.

Bursaries — for financial need

Bursaries are based on need, not marks, and you generally apply after you have been admitted and registered, through the school's own financial aid office. Most institutions open applications in September or October.

They are frequently under-claimed, because students assume they will not qualify or do not know they exist. If money is tight, apply. Many schools also hold emergency bursary funds for students whose circumstances change mid-year.

Applying for OSAP first usually helps, since many bursaries use your OSAP assessment as evidence of need.

Please read: this page does not assess, approve or guarantee any funding. The only figures it produces are arithmetic on numbers you enter yourself. OSAP eligibility and amounts are determined solely by the Ministry through your application, and scholarship and bursary decisions rest with the awarding body. Always confirm with the institution or funder before making a financial decision.
