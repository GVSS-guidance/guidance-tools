Personality type & learning preference — worth a look, with a caveat
Treat these two as conversation starters, not directions. Personality type results are known to be unstable — a lot of people get a different type when they retake the test a few weeks later. And the idea that you learn better when teaching is matched to a “visual”, “auditory” or “kinesthetic” style has repeatedly failed to hold up in research. They are interesting to reflect on. Neither should rule a career in or out for you.

    Where this list comes from, and why there is no score

    Step 2 comes mainly from the OECD Career Readiness project, which followed large groups of young people from about age 15 into their mid-twenties across eight countries and looked at which teenage activities went with better outcomes later. It confirmed 11 indicators. Worth knowing: Canada produced the most consistent evidence of any country in the study.

    These are associations, not guarantees. Nobody has shown that doing these things causes a better career, and this page does not claim they will. The evidence also differs item by item — some are confirmed indicators, some had only partial support, and a few (talking to your counsellor, campus tours, researching costs) are simply what this department sees work. The report itself notes that surprisingly few of the surveys asked anything about guidance counsellors, which is a gap in the data rather than a finding against them.

    There is no score because a score would mostly measure your circumstances. The same research records that these activities are unevenly distributed — students from better-off families do more of them. Someone working long hours to help at home, or who arrived in Canada recently, or whose family has no contacts in the field they are curious about, would come out “low” through nothing they did. So this page counts what you have done and never divides it by anything.

    Source: OECD (2021), Indicators of teenage career readiness: guidance for policy makers, Education Policy Perspectives No. 43, and Covacevich et al. (2021), Education Working Papers No. 258. OECD Career Readiness project ↗

    Who arranges what — please read before setting anything up

    The school organises and supervises co-op, SHSM and OYAP. Those are school programs, with the supervision and coverage that goes with them. If you want workplace experience that the school is responsible for, that is the route — ask your counsellor.

    Everything else on this page you arrange yourself. Job shadowing, workplace visits, informational chats and internships you find privately are set up by you and your family. Georges Vanier and the TDSB do not arrange, supervise, insure or take responsibility for them. Your counsellor is glad to talk through who to approach and what to ask — that is advice, not an arrangement.

    Talk to a parent or guardian before you set up a visit, and let them know where you are going and who you are meeting.

    What this page is not

    It does not assess you, test you or predict your future. It organises what you tell it and suggests what is worth doing next. Nothing here replaces a conversation with your counsellor, and no result from myBlueprint or any other survey should decide what you are capable of.
